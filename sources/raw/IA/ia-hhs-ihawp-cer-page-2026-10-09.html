IHAWP Community Engagement Requirements

About

By December 1, 2026, certain Iowa Health and Wellness Plan (IHWAP) members must meet monthly requirements to enroll and maintain coverage. These requirements are designed to encourage work and community involvement. Community engagement requirements apply to adults aged 19 to 64 who don't meet an exclusion.

Back to top

How to Meet New Requirements 

 There are several ways to meet these new requirements, including working, participating in an education or work program, completing community service, or combining different activities.

List items for IHAWP Work Requirement Activities

You must either:

  • Work at least 80 hours per month, or
  • Earn at least $580 per month based on the federal minimum wage.*

Work can be:

  • Paid work
  • Work in exchange for goods or services (in-kind work)
  • Unpaid work

Seasonal workers: Income is averaged over six months.

Enroll in a qualifying job training or work program for 80+ hours per month.

Enrolling in an education program at least half-time meets work requirements. Education programs include:

  • High school GED program at a community college
  • Community college
  • Vocational and technical school
  • 4-year colleges and universities leading to a bachelor’s degree
  • 1-2 year programs leading to a master’s or higher-level degree

Community service through unpaid work with a structured program completed for the benefit of the community meets work requirement activities. Community service hours do not have to be voluntary. They may be court ordered.

Community service cannot be partisan activities, benefit specific individuals, or be purely recreational in nature.

Examples of community service locations:

  • Schools
  • Humane society
  • Libraries
  • Food banks
  • Park volunteer

You can meet the requirement by completing a combination of activities totaling at least 80 hours per month, including:

  • Work
  • Education or training
  • Work programs
  • Community service
Back to top

Exclusions 

Some people on the IHAWP program are excluded from these new requirements.

These groups include: 

  • American Indians/Alaska Natives
  • Those under 19 or over 64
  • Individuals who are medically frail or otherwise have special medical needs 
    • NOTE: The state may require medical documentation for the medically frail exclusion.
  • Pregnant or had a baby in the last 12 months while on Iowa Medicaid
  • Caregivers of children under 14 or someone with a disability
  • Participating in a drug or alcohol treatment program
  • Participants in SNAP or FIP work programs
  • Veterans rated as totally disabled
  • Individuals currently or recently in jail or prison

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

List items for Community Engagement Requirements

For New Applicants: 

These requirements take effect December 1, 2026, for individuals who apply on or after that date. 

Applicants must meet the requirement for the month immediately preceding their application month.

For Current Members: 

These requirements apply to active IHAWP members with renewal dates on or after February 2027. 

Members must meet the requirement for any two months during the review period since their last eligibility determination or renewal.

If the requirement applies to you, you must spend at least 80 hours each month on one or more of these activities:

  • Work: Work for 80 hours per month or earn at least $580 per month (based on federal minimum wage).* Income from seasonal work may be averaged over six months.
  • Job Training: Take part in a qualifying job training or work program for 80 hours per month.
  • Education: Enrolled in a high school, college, GED, or other career and technical programs at least half-time.
  • Community Service: Volunteer work for 80 hours per month.

You can combine different activities to reach your 80 hours.

These groups include: 

  • American Indians/Alaska Natives
  • Those under 19 or over 64
  • Individuals who are medically frail or otherwise have special medical needs 
    • NOTE: The state may require medical documentation for the medically frail exclusion.
  • Pregnant or had a baby in the last 12 months while on Iowa Medicaid
  • Caregivers of children under 14 or someone with a disability
  • Participating in a drug or alcohol treatment program
  • Participants in SNAP or FIP work programs
  • Veterans rated as totally disabled
  • Individuals currently or recently in jail or prison

  • Check your mail and email. Iowa HHS will send you a letter or email in the upcoming months if the eligibility changes apply to you.
  • Track your activities. Keep track of any work, volunteer, job training, or school hours you complete each month. Start now so you’ll be ready to show that you meet the new requirement when it’s time to renew your coverage.
  • Gather documents. Gather documents in case you need to show proof that you meet the community engagement requirement or that you qualify for an exclusion.

Participants can meet the community engagement requirements by completing approved volunteer hours. Volunteer Iowa’s Get Connected platform provides an easy way to find and sign up for local volunteer opportunities that qualify toward these requirements.

Iowa HHS will check records to see if you already meet the requirements or are excluded. 

If HHS needs more proof, they will send you a letter telling you what to send.

If the new requirement applies to you and you don’t meet it, your Medicaid coverage may be denied or discontinued.

If this happens:

  • You can apply again at any time. Visit Iowa’s Self Service Portal at https://hhsservices.iowa.gov/apspssp/ssp.portal to get started if you lose coverage.
  • You might be able to get health coverage through the Health Insurance Marketplace®, but you might not qualify for financial help to lower your monthly premium. Visit HealthCare.gov to find or enroll in a health plan or call 1-800-318-2596 (TTY: 1-855-889-4325).
